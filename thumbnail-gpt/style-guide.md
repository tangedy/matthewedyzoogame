# Roblox thumbnail style guide

The full art rules. The GPT's instructions carry the short version; this file is the reference it reads for detail.

## 1. The Roblox look

- The image is a view from inside a finished, premium Roblox game: an in-game screenshot or high-end Roblox render. Never a photo of someone playing, never a phone, monitor, desk or real hand.
- Surfaces are smooth matte or lightly glossy plastic in flat solid colour. A wall is one clean plane; the ground is one continuous smooth surface. Patterns are printed flat on the surface.
- Buildings, vehicles, machines and scenery are each one smooth, whole object with a clean silhouette.
- Lighting is simple real-time lighting: one key light, soft sky fill and soft contact shadows where things meet the ground. Detail comes from shapes and poses, not effects layered on top. When in doubt, render it plainer.
- It must never look like a 3D animated film, a flat cartoon, a hand drawing, a sketch or a photograph.

## 2. Roblox avatars

An avatar is tall and slim: about three times taller than it is wide. Pick one body type per image and never mix them.

**Classic (default)**
- Box torso, the same width at the shoulders and waist.
- Arms: long straight rectangular blocks, about as long as the torso, even width end to end. They end in a flat square face with no hand.
- Legs: two long rectangular blocks with a gap between them, ending flat.

**Rounded (only when asked)**
- The same tall proportions with soft, segmented limbs.
- Simple rounded mitten hands and simple shoes.

**Both types**
- Head: a small smooth block, slightly wider than tall, about the width of the torso, sitting right on it with no neck.
- Face: a flat decal printed on the front of the head. Two simple eyes, one simple mouth, with a big exaggerated expression.
- Hair and hats: solid moulded plastic shells with clean edges.
- Clothing: printed onto the blocks in bold saturated colours, with few colours per outfit. Accessories are simple attached plastic pieces.
- "Hands on head" or "holding X" means the arm ends go there, with the face still toward the camera.

An avatar is wrong if it has short stubby limbs, a big round head, real fingers, a sculpted 3D face, flowing hair, draping cloth or realistic anatomy.

## 3. Composition: the squint test

Picture the thumbnail shrunk to the width of a thumb and blurred. The viewer must still see the main subject and what is happening in under one second.

- **One dominant subject**, 40-60% of the frame height, clearly the largest thing.
- **3-4 characters at most.** A row of same-sized, evenly spaced characters has no focal point.
- **A named special character** (boss, monster, rare animal, winner) is bigger, closer or lit apart from the rest.
- **Separated silhouettes.** Leave space so outlines never merge.
- **Strong value contrast.** A light subject on a darker background, or the reverse.
- **One background cue** (a barn, a sky, a fence line). No scattered props.
- **At most one framing element** (a doorway, a fence post, a truck edge), only if it helps the subject read.

## 4. Stopping power

- **Colour:** one dominant saturated hue paired with its opposite: orange/teal, purple/yellow, red/cyan, magenta/lime. Never muted, grey or muddy overall.
- **Contrast:** the subject is the brightest or darkest thing in the frame, with one rim light on the subject only.
- **Angle:** a low angle looking up (power), a high angle looking down (danger, or showing a layout) or a slight tilt (energy). Never flat and straight on at eye level.
- **Crop:** get close. The subject or a foreground element may run off the frame edge.
- **Moment:** just before or during the peak: mid-spray, mid-shake, mid-reveal. Never after.
- **Face:** the main character's expression carries the click. Make it huge and aim it at the viewer or the action.

Click killers: a symmetrical static row of characters, a grey palette, a dark muddy scene, a tiny subject lost in scenery, a background as busy as the subject.

## 5. Effects

Use only effects that explain the mechanic, kept near the focal point and never covering faces: water spray, mud splats and drips, soap foam and bubbles, sparkles on clean surfaces, coins and cash, upgrade arrows, rarity auras, speed lines, impact bursts.

Leave these out unless the genre needs them: haze, god rays, lens flare, chromatic aberration, vignette, heavy bloom, floating dust and background depth-of-field blur. Horror may use fog and moonlight; an RPG boss may use light rays.

## 6. Text (only when requested)

- Spell it exactly as given, with the same capitalisation.
- Style: chunky rounded bold letters with a white or bright yellow fill, a thick black outline and a slight drop shadow. This is the Roblox simulator look.
- Place it top-left, top-centre or bottom-centre, inside the middle 80% of the image height, never over the main face.
- When recreating text from a reference image, match its wording, font, size, colour, outline and placement.

## 7. Genre cues

- **Simulator** (default for Wash The Farm Animals): bright and cheerful, coins, upgrade arrows, before/after or weak/strong contrasts, a satisfying payoff.
- **Pet simulator:** pastels plus gold, rare pets, rarity glows, a reward chest.
- **Tycoon:** a half-built business, machines, money flowing, visible growth.
- **Obby:** mid-jump over lava or a gap, with a visible goal.
- **Horror:** dark blues and cold greens, a looming threat, scared faces, no gore.
- **Action/Sports:** speed lines, an object flying at the camera, a low angle.
- **RPG:** a huge boss facing small heroes, magic effects.

## 8. Format

- Wide landscape, full bleed, with no borders, black bars, frames or a smaller picture on a background.
- The image is cropped to 16:9 afterwards (1920x1080), so keep everything important inside the middle 80% of the height.
